*Question 2 - ridge regression
*a)
elasticnet logit chd sbp tobacco ldl i.famhist obesity alcohol age/*
*/, alpha(0) rseed(723963)
estimates store ridgecv
*From the output the best lambda choice (selected lambda) is .0493792

*b) 
cvplot
*This shows that as lambda decreases (note the reverse axis) the deviance decreases
* up until the point indicated by the red line. This is where, according to cross-validation,
*the deviance is minimized. The line to the right is not steep, suggesting the
*penalisation is not improving model performance that much here.

*c) 
coefpath, xunits(lnlambda) legend(on)
*As lambda increases, the coefficients are shrunk closer and closer to zero.
*Looking at the legend, we see that there are two coefficients for family
*history, even though it has only two levels. Normally if we include a binary
*covariate we get an intercept corresponding to the reference/baseline levels
*of the covariate + one coefficient for additional amount when you move to the
*next level of the covariate. Stata is doing something different here...

*If you want to add on the point where lambda is optimal:
coefpath, xunits(lnlambda) rawcoefs legend(on) xline(.0493792)

*d)
lassocoef logit ridgecv
lassocoef logit ridgecv, display(coef)
*For family history for the logit model we see that Absent is the base (reference)
*level. But for the ridge regression, we have a coefficient for both levels of 
*family history, in line with the coefpath plot earlier. We see that the Present
*coefficient is exactly minus the value of the Absent coefficient.

*e)
lassocoef logit, display(coef)
lassocoef ridgecv, display(coef, penalized)
*comparing the coefficients, we see that, as expected, the covariate effects
*are shrunk towards zero by ridge regression.

*f)
*We re-run the ordinary logistic so we can get the fitted probabilities
logit chd sbp tobacco ldl i.famhist obesity alcohol age
predict logitpr, pr
*And then re-run the ridge with same seed as before
elasticnet logit chd sbp tobacco ldl i.famhist obesity alcohol age/*
*/, alpha(0) rseed(723963)
predict ridgepr, pr
*And lastly summarise the two
summ logitpr ridgepr
*We see first that on average the two methods give identical probabilties on average.
*Next, we see that the SD of the model probabilities are larger for the logit
*model compared to the ridge logit - the effect of shrinkage on the coefficients
*is to shrink individual model predicted probabilities towards the overall mean.
*We also see the same in the min and max, which are somewhat less extreme for ridge.

*Question 3 - lasso regression
*a) 
lasso logit chd sbp tobacco ldl i.famhist obesity alcohol age, rseed(723963)
estimates store lassocv
*The output shows that at the selected lambda value, 5 non-zero coefficients have been
*retained out of the 8 original ones (7 covariates plus the extra one for family history).

*b)
coefpath, xunits(lnlambda) legend(on) xline(.0190284)
*From the plot we can see alcohol is zero throughout, obesity goes to zero quickly, and
*then SBP does too. These are in line with the p-values from the logistic, with these
*three covariates having large p-values.

*c)
lassocoef ridgecv lassocv, display(coef, penalized)
*This output confirms more clearly than the plot which covariates have been retained/dropped.
*For the family history variable we see lasso has dropped the indicator variable corresponding to 
*Present and retained the Absent indicator. So effectively the final model takes the reference
*level as Present and includes an indicator covariate for Absent.

*d)
lasso logit chd sbp tobacco ldl i.famhist obesity alcohol age/*
*/, selection(bic)
estimates store lassobic
lassocoef ridgecv lassocv lassobic, display(coef, penalized)
*Lasso with CV using the deviance selected 0.019 for lambda.
*Using the BIC we have a value of 0.0158, so a little smaller.
*This means we should get less shrinkage in the coefficients, and
*looking at the output from lassocoef this is indeed what we see.
